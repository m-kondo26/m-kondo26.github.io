# Reproducibility record

## Scientific scope

This project is a simplified geometry-plus-assumed-longitudinal-weighting reference. It is intended to make the following chain inspectable:

`direct acquired view -> ideal fan-beam complementary angle -> acquired lower/upper angular neighbors -> longitudinal bracketing within each direct/complementary union -> convex angular combination -> acquisition-geometry kernel and per-view moments -> configured-thickness explanatory SSPz`

It is not an implementation of a commercial CT reconstruction algorithm. In particular, it does not infer proprietary projection selection, redundancy weighting, cone-beam backprojection, iterative reconstruction, deep-learning reconstruction, noise, temporal sensitivity, or motion response.

Version 2026-08-28.2 uses the fan-beam 180LI acquisition geometry as its default explanatory SSPz path. Direct acquired views are sampled at equally spaced relative tube angles `beta=2*pi*k/N`, `k=0,...,N-1`, over a half-open 0-to-360-degree turn. Each direct view is paired with the two acquired complementary views bracketing the ideal angle `beta+pi+2*gamma`. The former one-family 0-to-360-degree direct-ray full-scan calculation remains selectable as a comparator; no previous half-scan SSPz, FWHM, or FWTM value is reused. Version 2026-08-28.2 retains the pre-weight, all-row acquisition-geometry curve introduced in 2026-08-28.1 and makes the same all-row candidate-pool contract explicit in the two-level unwrapped diagram.

The 180LI model computes the fan-beam complementary source position for every acquired direct-view angle. With source position `S(beta)` on the radius-`R` orbit and fixed evaluation point `P`, the ray direction is `D=P-S`. The second intersection with the source orbit is `S_c=S+tD`, where `t=-2(S dot D)/(D dot D)`. The polar angle of `S_c` is `beta_c`; its forward angular separation from `beta` is equivalent to `180 degrees + 2 gamma` under the displayed fan-angle sign convention. At the isocenter, and when fan/cone scaling is disabled, the separation reduces exactly to 180 degrees. The implementation records the acquired views immediately before and after the ideal `beta_c`, their signed angular residuals, and the linear angular interpolation fraction `lambda`. These two acquired angular branches are applied to the explanatory SSPz rather than being display-only.

For the longitudinal audit, view families are retained in absolute helical acquisition order, `D_n -> C_n -> D_(n+1)`, where `D` denotes the direct-ray family and `C` the complementary-ray family. The arrows denote acquisition order rather than longitudinal order. Two constrained cross-family spans are evaluated separately: `D_n -> C_n` and `C_n -> D_(n+1)`. For each absolute-view pair, all detector rows and the required ideal-helix turns are searched in both longitudinal orientations, and the minimum interval whose endpoints lie on opposite sides of the target plane is retained. Showing both spans prevents the complementary-angle correction from being reduced to the distance of only one member of a pair.

For each acquired angular branch, the generalized two-point-LI calculation merges all longitudinal samples from the direct and complementary families, sorts them by z, and selects the adjacent candidates immediately below and above `z0`. The two endpoints are retained jointly: if `d-` and `d+` are their distances from `z0`, the bracket is `G=d-+d+`; neither endpoint is treated as a sufficient metric on its own. An ordinary selected pair can therefore be direct-direct (DD), direct-complementary (DC), complementary-direct (CD), or complementary-complementary (CC). Equal-z ties share the endpoint weight equally as a neutral geometric convention, and an exact match receives total weight one. The lower- and upper-angular branch kernels are then combined as `(1-lambda)K_L + lambda K_U`. This follows the acquisition-side sampling logic described by Taguchi and Aradate and by Hu. The constrained cross-family spans remain diagnostics and are not averaged as an unvalidated redundancy rule.

These calculations reproduce the declared idealized acquisition geometry, including continuous `180 degrees + 2 gamma`, acquired-view angular bracketing, linear angular combination, absolute view order, all detector rows, and unlimited ideal-helix turn search. They do not reproduce scanner-specific detector-channel interpolation, projection selection beyond the declared two angular neighbors, redundancy weighting, cone-beam weighting, z filtering, or backprojection. Accordingly, the output is an `180LI acquisition-geometry explanatory model`, not a complete commercial 180LI reconstruction.

For the idealized cone-geometry condition, the source-to-evaluation-point distance ratio is `q(beta)=L(beta)/R=sqrt(1+(r/R)^2-2(r/R)cos(beta))`, where `R` is source-to-isocenter distance, `r` is the transverse distance from isocenter to the fixed evaluation point, `beta=0` places the source in the evaluation-point direction, and `L(beta)` is source-to-point distance. The candidate-row center is the sum of the linear helical table-motion term and the detector-row offset scaled by `q(beta)`. Therefore the on-condition traces bend continuously over a rotation and return to the same distance scale at 360 degrees. The distance ratio increases from 0 to 180 degrees and decreases from 180 to 360 degrees; the signed deviation from the parallel-beam approximation, `q-1`, crosses zero twice. This is a first-order geometric projection model, not a complete cone-beam reconstruction.

Within each of the two angular branches, the calculation searches the ideal infinite helix for the nearest candidate with a longitudinal coordinate smaller than `z0` and the nearest candidate with a longitudinal coordinate larger than `z0`. These are the two sides of the longitudinal `z` coordinate; they do not denote the upper and lower directions of the unwrapped diagram. The two candidates receive linear interpolation weights whose sum is one and whose first moment about `z0` is zero. No fixed longitudinal support or fixed number of turns can invalidate an otherwise reconstructable ideal-helix state. If the branch gaps are `G_L` and `G_U`, the path-audit indicator is `G_eff/T=[(1-lambda)G_L+lambda G_U]/T`. The individual branch gaps remain available in numerical output. The comparator-path audit is its one-family `delta-z/T`. These indicators describe the selected nearest-bracketing path only; they are not measures of the spread of every final contribution. Scanner-specific redundancy weighting, cone-beam backprojection, and proprietary z-filtering remain outside scope.

For the reader-facing geometry curve, each direct-view angle `beta` defines a finite, pre-weight candidate set. It contains every one of the `N` row centres in the direct acquired view and every row centre in the acquired lower and upper angular neighbours bracketing the ideal complementary angle. Repeated physical views are included only once, so the set contains `2N` points when the ideal complementary angle is itself acquired and `3N` points otherwise. If their longitudinal positions are `z_k`, their arithmetic mean is `z_bar`, and their count is `M`, the displayed value is the population standard deviation `sqrt(sum_k((z_k-z_bar)^2)/M)` in millimetres. The calculation uses no interpolation weight, nearest-point selection, row-aperture variance, support threshold, or configured slice thickness `T`. A common longitudinal translation of every candidate leaves the value unchanged; consequently reconstruction-plane position `s` would only repeat the same curve and is not shown.

The weighted moments and final nonzero-candidate counts used internally by the explanatory SSPz path remain available for numerical regression. They are not relabelled as the pre-weight geometry curve and are not displayed in the reader-facing Section 3C. In particular, the former `sigma_g/T` and `K(beta,s)` panels are superseded because they mixed later candidate selection or weighting with the acquisition-geometry question addressed here.

The reader-facing order is: the acquisition-geometry explanation, the angle-dependent unweighted axial spread curve, the configured-output 360-state centre-shape overlay, a separate configured-output low-amplitude-tail overlay, and scalar width summaries. The pre-thickness intermediate SSPz profile and its sampled FWHM/FWTM are not exposed in public figures, publication PNGs, or CSV files. The state-sweep CSV does retain scalar acquisition-geometry-kernel audits from the explanatory SSPz calculation, including `mean_geometry_kernel_second_moment_mm2` and `analytic_base_sigma_mm`; they are explicitly labelled as internal model audits and are not the new pre-weight curve. The curve uses every acquired relative tube-angle view `beta` from 0 degrees inclusive to 360 degrees exclusive, without angular decimation. The public input is configured slice thickness, not effective FWHM. No model parameter is fitted to the computed FWHM; FWHM, FWTM, standard deviation, centroid, and configured-output profile shape are outputs.

The 180LI acquisition-geometry CSV uses a long format with four records per direct-ray acquired view: the continuous ideal complementary angle, the acquired views immediately below and above that angle, and the nearest acquired view. It records both absolute-view-constrained spans and both endpoints of the generalized adjacent pair. Because the continuous ideal angle is not itself an acquired view, its absolute-view fields are intentionally blank rather than encoded with a numeric sentinel.

For each entered condition, the numerical SSPz grid uses one state- and cone-condition-independent longitudinal domain large enough to contain one table feed, the maximum idealized row aperture, and the configured-thickness window. The user-selected grid count is a minimum. The implementation targets a cell width no larger than `min(rowWidth/16,T/64)`, increases the count to the next odd value so that one cell center lies at `z=0`, and caps the internal count at 65,535 with an explicit cap-hit audit flag. Candidate rectangles are rasterized as cell averages using their exact overlap length with every intersected z cell; rectangle endpoints are not rounded to integer cell indices. The configured-thickness operation likewise integrates the piecewise-constant cell profile over the exact moving-window intersection, including fractional boundary cells. Thus area and centroid are not shifted by endpoint bin rounding; remaining error is the declared finite piecewise-constant grid approximation. This prevents a changing numerical search window or cell-index convention from being mistaken for geometry-driven SSPz variation. The calculation domain is not reused as the visible domain. The selected-state detail and 360-state center-shape charts use symmetric outward-rounded axes derived only from configured-output values at or above 10% of peak. A separate logarithmic tail chart uses cone-geometry-scaled configured-output values at or above 0.1%. The center off/on panels share horizontal limits; the single tail panel derives its horizontal limits only from the retained cone-geometry-scaled curves.

Because each view's longitudinal first moment is constrained to zero and direct-view integration is equally weighted in this explanatory model, the variance of the view-averaged pre-thickness geometry kernel is `mean_view(M2)`. Convolution with the centered configured-thickness rectangle of width `T` adds its variance `T^2/12`, giving the internal analytic identity `sigma_configured^2=mean_view(M2)+T^2/12`. The implementation reports the analytic value and the residual against the numerically rasterized configured-output standard deviation. This identity verifies the declared model; it does not assert the SSPz of a commercial scanner.

The same version removes relative reconstruction position, reference z position, state-sweep count, and diagram turn count from the acquisition-condition form. The full 360-state set is automatic. A result-side selector chooses one of those states only for detailed inspection. The unwrapped overview automatically covers the finite turn set inspected by the SSPz candidate search, retains every detector row, and stores shared parametric trace arrays rather than a nested point array for every row/turn trajectory. The radial-position interface spans 0--250 mm; 250 mm is identified as the geometric edge of a 500-mm field of view, not as a guarantee of a scanner-specific usable field.

The 360-profile overlay uses states `s=k/360`, `k=0,...,359`, within one table feed per rotation. It is not a set of measured absolute tube angles or 360 repeated acquisitions. All 360 configured-output states are rendered as 360 separate thin paths without state decimation or an overlaid pointwise summary curve or band. Each profile is peak-normalized and retained on its native `z-z0` coordinate without peak, centroid, or half-height-midpoint alignment. The pointwise configured-output maximum is retained internally only for threshold-based axis derivation and is not drawn. Full-resolution configured-output profiles are used for scalar metrics. If the SSPz grid contains more than 1000 samples, only the within-profile canvas z-grid is uniformly reduced to at most 1000 samples and this fact is written inside the exported image; the 360 states themselves are never reduced.

The publication renderer follows one shared axis contract. Axes and text are black; support grids are light gray; units appear in parentheses; all labels on one axis use the same number of decimal places. Tick steps are selected from the natural `1, 2, 5 x 10^n` sequence. Axis limits are expanded outward to integer multiples of that step and never rounded inward across rendered data. Thus an extent near 132 mm may be shown as 150 mm, while an 80-row condition whose complete candidate extent exceeds 300 mm is shown at 400 mm rather than being clipped to 150 mm. Major ticks point outward, minor midpoint ticks point inward, and the main axes use at least five divisions. Comparable SSPz panels share their horizontal domain.

The publication PNG control re-renders the chart at its target physical size rather than resizing the screen canvas. Panel figures are exported at 80 mm and 1890 pixels; full-width figures are exported at 180 mm and 4252 pixels. Both correspond to 600 dpi after rounding, and a PNG `pHYs` chunk records the physical resolution. Publication mode omits the interactive selected-state marker and redundant title/subtitle text while retaining the axes, data, and essential encoding legend; detailed conditions belong in the figure caption. These rules are based on the current Medical Physics author instructions and the Wiley electronic-artwork guidance; final manuscript tables remain editable rather than being submitted as screenshots.

## Numerical regression

Run:

```powershell
npm test
```

The comparator-path regression loads `tests/full-scan-reference.json` and compares the JavaScript core with the independent NumPy implementation in `tests/full_scan_reference.py`. The fixed condition is 4 detector rows, 1.0-mm row width, beam pitch 0.875, 600-mm source-to-isocenter distance, 102-mm radial position, relative reconstruction position 0.5 within one table feed, 360 actual tube-angle views over 0-to-360 degrees, and 800 SSPz samples. The independent reference contains no direct/complementary-ray conversion and is intentionally restricted to the legacy comparator.

The 102-mm radial position is retained only as a study-derived numerical regression fixture. It is not presented as a general CT reference radius or as a recommended public preset. The public interface uses generic presets of 0, 50, 100, 150, 200, and 250 mm.

The comparator regression checks FWHM, FWTM, standard deviation, centroid, angular coverage, bracketing gap, and `delta-z/T` with and without idealized source-to-evaluation-point distance scaling. The independent reference uses the same exact fractional cell-overlap definition for unit-area row rectangles and exact integration of the piecewise-constant configured-thickness window. It checks full-profile agreement, area conservation, centroid, and configured-output metrics. Current acceptance values are stored in the machine-readable fixture rather than duplicated as rounded constants here. The test also requires the pre-thickness acquisition kernel and its z grid to remain identical when only configured thickness changes.

An 80-row, 0.5-mm-row-width, 250-mm-radius condition is evaluated at all 360 relative reconstruction states. Every state must retain 100% ideal-helix angular coverage and a finite intermediate FWHM. This specifically guards against the superseded fixed-support behavior that produced a missing central interval. A separate periodicity assertion verifies that source-to-evaluation-point distance increases from 0 to 180 degrees and decreases from 180 to 360 degrees.

A separate comparator-path public-output regression uses 160 rows, 0.5-mm row width, beam pitch 1.35, 600-mm source-to-isocenter distance, 0-mm radial position, 1.0-mm configured thickness, 1200 direct-ray full-scan views, and 2000 requested longitudinal samples. This is explicitly the legacy comparator, not an acceptance target for the default 180LI path. Its reader-facing width must remain the configured-output result and must not fall back to the narrower internal pre-thickness response.

The authoritative file hashes and fixture parameters are recorded in `model-manifest.json`.

The unwrapped-diagram display is tested separately from the numerical SSPz regression. In both the no-cone reference and the cone-geometry-scaled condition, Section 2A renders the centres of all `N` detector rows in every displayed acquired view as the candidate-pool trajectories. The no-cone reference fixes the angle-dependent row-offset scale to one; it does not restrict the population to central rows or to rows within configured slice thickness `T`. Section 2B retains the same all-row trajectories and overlays circles only at the nearest interpolation endpoints selected from that population. `T` is not used to exclude candidates or to select those endpoints; it is applied later to the assembled acquisition-geometry kernel when producing configured-output SSPz. Tests require the candidate identities and selected endpoint geometry to remain unchanged when only `T` changes, one detector-row trajectory per displayed turn, selected endpoints in both angular halves, unit within-view weight sums on both sides of 180 degrees, and a periodic relation in which the 360-degree trace endpoint equals the 0-degree geometry shifted by one table feed.

The primary 180LI regression requires: an exact 180-degree separation at isocenter and in the parallel-beam reference; agreement with the independent fan-angle formula; a second-intersection orbit residual below `1e-9 mm`; one result for every direct acquired view; and lower/upper angular neighbors that bracket the ideal complementary angle. For every view, each branch's longitudinal weights must sum to one with zero first moment about `z0`; the angular weights must also sum to one; the final per-view contribution must sum to one; and the SSPz must repeat after one table-feed state period. An independent small-condition oracle reconstructs the complementary mapping, detector-row/turn search, final candidate weights, and all-contribution `M2` without calling the production search helpers. The branch gaps returned by the SSPz calculation are cross-checked against independently retained 180LI audit arrays, and the numerical configured-output variance is checked against `mean_view(M2)+T^2/12`. The absolute-view-pair regression continues to verify `D_n -> C_n -> D_(n+1)` ordering and treats those two constrained spans as diagnostics rather than SSPz weights.

`tests/lattice-equivalence.mjs` independently restores the exhaustive all-row/two-nearest-turn search that preceded the optimized analytic lattice search. Six fixtures spanning 1--320 rows, beam pitch 0.25--2.5, radius 0--249.9 mm, and both geometry conditions compare 1440 direct-view cases. The direct gap, both 180LI branch gaps, and the angularly weighted effective gap must agree within `3e-6 mm`. This test demonstrates that the speed optimization is an equivalent search, not candidate decimation or an approximation.

`tests/all-candidate-axial-spread.mjs` independently enumerates every row centre in the direct and distinct complementary-neighbour acquired views and compares that population with the production closed-form grouped variance. It verifies exact invariance to configured slice thickness `T`, reconstruction-plane state `s`, and a common one-turn longitudinal translation; equality of cone-on and cone-off results at `r=0`; the `2N` exactly acquired and `3N` off-grid complementary-angle cases; and the periodic equality of 0 and 360 degrees. This test is separate from the later explanatory-SSPz weighting regression.
